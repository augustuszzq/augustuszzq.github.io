About

I am currently a Ph.D. student at Stevens Institute of Technology. Before starting my Ph.D., I received an M.S. in Computer Science from Johns Hopkins University and a B.Eng. in Electronic and Information Engineering from Imperial College London. I have also worked as a research assistant at Argonne National Laboratory and as a software engineer in industry.

My work is motivated by a simple question: as AI systems become more compound, interactive, and tool-driven, what kinds of runtime abstractions and control mechanisms are needed to make them dependable in practice? I approach this question from a systems perspective, combining runtime design, performance analysis, and careful empirical evaluation.

Research

My recent research can be organized around three closely related themes.

Runtime Governance for Agentic AI

I study governance mechanisms for tool-augmented and agentic AI systems, with an emphasis on communication bottlenecks, runtime control, scheduling, and observability.

LLM Inference and Serving Systems

I work on efficient LLM decoding and serving, including strict-static execution, KV-cache management, transport-aware runtime design, and performance under heterogeneous workloads.

Benchmarking and Systems Evaluation

I am interested in building reproducible evaluations for AI systems and accelerator platforms, with attention to measurement quality, bottleneck attribution, and realistic workload characterization.
